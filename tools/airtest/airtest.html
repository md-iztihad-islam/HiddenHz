HiddenHz air test

Can a picture hidden at 15–22 kHz survive a speaker, a room and a microphone? Laptop A plays the test signal; laptop B records it and scores each band.

Laptop A — play

Set the system volume to what you would use in the demo (70–80 %). Turn off any "audio enhancements" or equaliser. About s.

Laptop B — record

Start recording first, then press play on laptop A. Stop when the tones end. Browser echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto gain are switched off here — they would filter out exactly the band we care about.

Live view, 7–23 kHz, bottom to top.

Result

Recording spectrogram, 7–23 kHz. Each band should show as a bright block of horizontal lines.

SNR is the level of one codec tone over the noise in the same DFT bin (n_fft 2048). The picture spans 30 dB of tone level (dynamic_db), so ≥35 dB keeps the darkest pixels above the noise; 25–35 dB gives a recognisable picture with crushed shadows; below 25 dB it breaks up. Tones are played at the level they would have in a payload-only mix at peak 0.9. The current file mix (payload_gain 0.06) is 23.5 dB quieter.
